2.1 Zero-Allocation Design Principles

The engine achieves sub-microsecond determinism by enforcing four inviolable coding rules: (1) Zero Heap Allocation on the Hot Path: No Vec, String, or dynamic boxing during pricing or order processing; stack arrays and pre-sized rings only. (2) Cache-Line Padding: Hot atomic variables are wrapped in CachePadded<T> to eliminate false-sharing across CPU cores. (3) Pre-Faulted SHM: Shared memory segments are pinned and pre-faulted via madvise(MADV_WILLNEED) at startup. (4) Pure Safe Rust: Enforced via #![forbid(unsafe_code)] across the entire demonstration suite.

3.2 Roger Lee (2004) Asymptotic Wing Bound Proof

A common failure of stochastic volatility and polynomial local volatility models is extreme wing arbitrage: generating implied variance that violates the moment formula of Roger Lee (2004). For log-moneyness k = \ln(K/F), total implied variance w(k, T) = \sigma^2(k, T) \cdot T must satisfy:

\limsup_{|k| \to \infty} \frac{w(k, T)}{|k|} \le 2.0

CelNet's engine analytically enforces this bound. Furthermore, the Breeden-Litzenberger risk-neutral density p(K) = \frac{\partial^2 C}{\partial K^2} \ge 0 is computed for every point, guaranteeing complete absence of calendar and butterfly arbitrage.

Standard execution models (Almgren-Chriss) assume instantaneous permanent impact. In modern high-frequency electronic markets, price impact is transient and decays according to a power-law kernel:

6.1 Hardware Pre-Faulted Lock-Free IPC (11.28 ns)

Inter-process communication (IPC) frameworks such as Aeron, gRPC, and Unix domain sockets suffer from kernel context switches, memory copies, and thread scheduling jitter.

Segments are pinned at allocation and protected against false-sharing via crossbeam_utils::CachePadded 64-byte alignment. Producers write sequence-tagged frames using atomic release semantics; consumers read with acquire barriers.
